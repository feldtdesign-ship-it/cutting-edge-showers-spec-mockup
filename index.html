What the tile setter and the builder should know so the enclosure is safe, stays clean and doesn’t leak.

Keeping it clear

Towel or squeegee the glass after every use. Left for weeks or months, water spots can become hard or impossible to remove, depending on your water chemistry. It varies from town to town. A glass sealant such as ClearShield cuts maintenance and cleaning time.

Never use
  • Abrasive or soft-abrasive powders and liquids
  • Bleach or bleach-based cleaners
  • Steel or Teflon pads
  1. Use 90°, 135° or 180° angles. Most shower hardware is built for them, which keeps the install cost-effective and the enclosure working properly.
  2. Pitch the curb toward the drain. A level curb holds water. A curb sloped away from the drain leaks onto the bathroom floor.
  3. Pitch saddles and bench seats inward so water runs off and doesn’t sit.
  4. Cap curbs with granite, marble or Corian instead of tile.
  5. Block the wall behind the tile with wood wherever a hinge or panel will mount.
  6. Keep glass tile away from hinges and clamps. Breakage is likely during and after install, which means delays and repair charges.
  7. Steam units need glass from curb to ceiling, so vent the inside of the shower to keep moisture under control.
  8. Keep body sprays and shower heads off the seams. Water pressure can push water past the sweeps and seals.
  9. Neo-angle with a knee wall: the door must meet the structure at 90° for the hinges. Glass-to-glass joints at 135° have no such limit.
  10. Leave the door clear. Raised decorative tile, overhanging tile, and stone slabs on a seat or buttress wall can block its swing or leave gaps.
